## Flagged gap 1 — is `preservationEO` an F1 tautology? **NO — verified by construction.**

Three executed experiments (all against the real built module):

1. **P1, definitional-collapse probe:** `example (e : Expr) : evalEOtgt EvalOrder.lr e = evalEO e := rfl`
   → **FAILS to typecheck** ("Type mismatch: rfl … expected evalEOtgt EvalOrder.lr e = evalEO e").
   `evalEOtgt lr` does NOT reduce to `evalEO` by definitional unfolding; they are separately
   compiled recursions. The theorem is not `rfl`-true.
2. **P2, IH-load-bearing probe:** the same statement by `induction` with the `add`/`seqS` arms
   closed by `simp only [evalEOtgt, evalEO]` WITHOUT `iha`/`ihb`
   → **FAILS** (unsolved goals in both arms; the goal visibly needs `evalEOtgt lr a ↦ evalEO a`).
   The IHs in the committed proof are load-bearing; the induction is real, not decorative.
3. **MUT1, bugged-`lr` mutant:** flipped the `lr` add success trace to `tb ++ ta`
   (the `evalEOtgt` occurrence only; reference untouched)
   → `preservationEO` itself **fails** (error at the theorem, line 201 of the mutant).
   The theorem text binds the `lr` arm's behavior: a wrong `lr` cannot satisfy it.

Nuance kept honest: the `lr` arm is, inevitably, a clause-for-clause transcription of the
reference — that is what "the compiler is correct" means at model tier. What saves it from
F1 is that (a) the transcription is a distinct recursion the kernel does not identify with
the reference (P1), and (b) any divergence in it is caught (MUT1). Since `preservationEO`
*is* extensional equality with the reference, NO subtly-wrong `lr` can satisfy it — the
only residual attack surface is the reference itself being wrong, addressed below.

## BLOCKER B1 — header coverage overstatement (doc-level; one-line fix)

`EffectOrder.lean` lines 26–27 claim slice 6c is "closing C5's language-owned part", and
lines 45–49 cite Python §6.16 *including the call-argument clause* ("each call argument is
evaluated before the … next") followed by "each faithfulness fact is pinned by a `#guard`
below".

- The fragment models exactly two binary constructs: `add` (binary operand order) and
  `seqS` (two-statement order). There is **no call node**, no n-ary argument list, no
  comparison chain, no tuple/list/dict display order, no assignment RHS-vs-target order.
- Therefore: (a) "closing C5's language-owned part" is false as stated — call-argument
  order (`f(g(), h())`), the single most common real-world manifestation of this bug
  class, remains unmodeled; (b) "each faithfulness fact is pinned by a `#guard`" is
  literally false for the call-argument clause the header itself cites — an unmodeled
  construct cannot be pinned.
- Why blocking rather than cosmetic: the header is the honesty surface downstream artifacts
  (paperC, TRUST) quote; per the shop's own no-overstatement discipline, a coverage claim
  broader than the fragment is a statement-strength defect even when every theorem is sound.
- Minimal fix (either): reword to "closing the synchronous binary-operand/statement-order
  core of C5" (or equivalent) and trim the §6.16 citation to the left-operand clause
  actually modeled; or extend the fragment with an n-ary `call` node + pins. The reworded
  claim is fully backed by what's proved; no theorem change needed.
